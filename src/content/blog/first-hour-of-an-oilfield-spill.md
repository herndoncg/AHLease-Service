How big a spill cleanup gets is mostly decided in the first hour. This is a plain checklist for the time between finding a release and a crew arriving. It does not replace your company's own procedure, which comes first.

## 1. Make it safe

Keep people and vehicles back, keep ignition sources away, and do not walk into anything you cannot identify.

## 2. Stop the source, if it is safe to

Close a valve, shut in a well or stop a transfer only if it can be done safely and you are trained to do it.

## 3. Call

Your supervisor, your company's spill contact, and a cleanup crew. For A&H, call 903-424-1607. Emergencies are answered 24/7. Have ready:

- The county, the lease name and the nearest road
- What released, if you know: oil, produced water, a chemical
- About how much, and whether it is still flowing
- Whether it is near a creek, pond, ditch or fence line

## 4. Slow the spread

If you have equipment and can do it safely, a berm or a shallow trench across the path of the liquid buys time. Keeping it out of a ditch or creek matters most.

## 5. Take photos

Photos of the source and the spread, with the time, help everyone: the cleanup crew, your company and the reporting.

## What happens next

The crew contains the release, recovers free liquid, removes affected soil and backfills once the cleanup is released. Reporting to the regulator stays with the operator.
