Most surprises on a dirt work invoice come from something nobody knew when the job was bid. The more of these you can send with a bid request, the closer the first number lands.

## 1. Where it is, exactly

The county and the lease name are a start. A pin, GPS coordinates or the location's legal description is better. It tells us the haul distance for rock, which is often the biggest single cost on a road or pad.

## 2. How big

Length, width and rock depth for a road. Length, width and depth for a pad. Miles and right-of-way width for mowing or spraying. If you are not sure, a rough number is fine; the [estimators](/estimators/) will show you how much the size moves the price.

## 3. What the ground is like

Open pasture or timber. Dry or wet. Flat or sloped. A couple of phone photos answer most of these.

## 4. What is already there

Old rock, culverts, fence, a cattle guard, buried lines. Anything existing can save money if it can be reused, or cost money if it has to come out.

## 5. When it has to be done

A road that has to be ready for a rig move in ten days is a different job from one that can wait for dry weather.

## 6. Who to call on site

The company man, foreman or landowner contact, so the crew is not waiting at a locked gate.

Send what you have through the [bid request form](/request-a-bid/). We will call with any questions and walk the location before a firm bid on anything bigger than a small repair.
