A right-of-way has to stay open enough to be walked, driven and seen from the air. In East Texas, with a growing season that runs from about March into October, grass alone can close one in a few months.

## Picking a schedule

**Two cuts a year** is the usual minimum: one in late spring after the first flush of growth, and one in late summer or fall. This keeps the line visible and stops brush from getting established.

**Three or four cuts** make sense where the right-of-way crosses improved pasture or runs near homes, where a landowner agreement calls for it, or around above-ground equipment that has to be reached often.

**A one-time cleanup cut** is a different job. Ground that has gone more than a season without a mower usually has brush in it, takes longer and costs more the first time. After that it can go on a regular schedule at a lower price per cut.

## When to spray instead

Mowing cuts brush off. It does not kill it, and most brush comes back thicker. Where brush keeps returning, selective spraying that takes out the brush and leaves the grass will stretch the time between cuts. Around risers, valve sites and inside firewalls, bare-ground spraying removes the need to trim by hand at all.

## What it costs

Mowing is priced by the acre, which comes from the length and width of the right-of-way. The [mowing estimator](/estimators#mowing) gives a range from the miles and the width.
