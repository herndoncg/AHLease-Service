A lease road rarely fails because a truck was too heavy. It fails because water got into it and stayed. Once the ground under the rock is wet, every loaded truck pumps mud up through the surface, and the rock you paid for disappears into the subgrade.

## The four things that keep a road standing

**A crown.** The road should be higher in the middle than at the edges, so rain runs off to the sides instead of sitting in the wheel tracks. A flat road is a pond waiting for a truck.

**Ditches that go somewhere.** Water shed off the crown needs a ditch to carry it away, and the ditch needs an outlet. A ditch that dead-ends just moves the pond to the side of the road.

**Dry, compacted ground under the rock.** Rock spread on soft or wet ground sinks. Cutting out the soft material and compacting the subgrade first is what makes the rock last. On the worst ground, fabric under the rock keeps the two from mixing.

**Enough rock for the traffic.** Four inches carries a pumper. Rig moves and frac traffic need six to eight inches or more.

## What maintenance buys you

Blading a road back to its crown and reopening the ditches before the wet season costs a fraction of rebuilding it afterward. If a road is starting to rut, that is the time to call, while the base under it is still sound.
